## 1. Brand Overview

Spotva is a marketplace that connects people who need a physical space with the people and businesses who have one to offer. A teacher needs a classroom for Saturday. A startup needs a meeting room for one afternoon. A podcaster needs a quiet room with good acoustics, once a week. A photographer needs a studio with natural light, for three hours. Spotva is where all of those searches end — not a coworking directory, not an office-rental listings site, but the general discovery and booking layer for *any* usable space.

Three groups meet on Spotva:

| Who | What they get |
|---|---|
| **Customers** | Teachers, trainers, tutors, coaches, consultants, freelancers, startups, companies, teams, event organizers, creators, photographers, podcasters, workshop hosts — anyone who needs a space, sometimes or often. |
| **Space providers** | Businesses and individuals offering training rooms, classrooms, meeting rooms, conference rooms, seminar rooms, coworking spaces, private offices, workshop spaces, event spaces, podcast/content rooms, and other bookable spaces. |
| **Partners** | Bloggers, influencers, marketers, community owners, and content creators who promote Spotva and earn commission on the bookings they bring. |

Model: **Customer → Spotva → Space Provider**, and **Partner → Spotva → Customer → Booking → Commission**.

Spotva is the transaction and discovery layer between demand and supply — not an online directory that happens to list rooms.

## 2. Brand Story

Every useful space starts as somebody else's spare capacity — an empty classroom on a Saturday, a meeting room nobody's booked for Thursday afternoon, a studio between shoots. And every good idea — a class, a workshop, a meeting, a shoot, an event — starts with the same unglamorous problem: finding a room that's actually free, actually right, and actually bookable without five phone calls.

Spotva exists to close that gap directly. Not by building more space, but by making the space that already exists *findable* and *bookable* in minutes — for whoever needs it, whatever they're doing with it. The name carries that idea on purpose: **Spot**, because this is fundamentally about place — the right one, found. **Va**, because what the platform actually delivers, transaction by transaction, is **v**alue drawn out of **a**ccess: providers unlock revenue from space that would otherwise sit empty; customers unlock possibility they couldn't act on without it; partners unlock income by connecting the two.

That "Value + Access" reading is the brand's internal logic, not a public tagline — see §3 for why it stays backstage.

---

## 3. Name Rationale

**Spotva = Spot + Va**

**Spot** — a physical place, location, or suitable space. It plugs directly into how people already talk about this need: *find a spot, book a spot, work spot, meeting spot, training spot, event spot, workshop spot, creative spot.* Critically, "spot" is category-agnostic — it does not say "desk" or "office," so it doesn't box Spotva into being read as *only* a coworking product (see §20 — this is the single most important long-term brand-expansion lever the name gives us).

**Va** — deliberately *not* a geographic reference (not "VA = Azerbaijan"). The intended internal reading is **Value + Access**: access to the right space, and the value that access creates for whoever uses it. This is backstory, not billboard copy — it earns its keep in brand strategy documents, partner onboarding narratives, and investor/press conversations, but the public-facing brand stays simple: **Spotva**, full stop, not visually or verbally split into "Spot" + "VA" in customer-facing contexts. See §10/§24 — no letterform, color, or weight change is used to separate the two halves anywhere the public sees the wordmark.

## 4. Positioning

**Primary positioning statement:**

> Spotva is a marketplace for discovering and booking the right space for work, learning, meetings, events, and more.

**Explicitly avoid** positioning Spotva as: a coworking company, an office rental company, or a room rental website. Those are all things Spotva *does*, none of which it *is*. The brand is built to expand internationally and into further categories of physical space (and eventually adjacent services — see §17), so nothing in messaging, visual identity, or product naming should imply a ceiling at "desks and meeting rooms."

## 8. Personality

**Spotva is:** modern, confident, approachable, intelligent, practical, energetic, trustworthy, premium (not luxurious), professional (not corporate), international, human.

**Spotva is not:** overly corporate, cold, generic SaaS, a cheap classified-ad site, overly playful, luxury real-estate, a traditional coworking brand, overly technical.

The visual identity (§10–§17) reads as **a modern marketplace**, not a property company — that distinction is the single most useful filter for judging any future asset (an ad, a landing page, a piece of signage): would a property-management company have made this, or would a marketplace?

## 10. Logo Concept — ✅ APPROVED: "S + Spot / Access Point"

**Revision history:** Concept A, "The Access Point" (a rounded square with a docking circle), was approved first and taken through full production artwork. Once built and viewed in finished form, the brand owner found it unconvincing and asked for a different direction: a mark built primarily from Spotva's own initial letter, "S," rather than a "sp" monogram. Three new S-based directions were explored and compared against seven named criteria (recognizability, scalability, favicon/app-icon suitability, marketplace/platform feel, international brand potential, visual distinctiveness, light/dark performance); the one below is what the brand owner selected. Concept A and Concept B (the brief's original two directions) both remain below for the record, marked not selected — their production files are kept in `docs/brand/assets/archive/concept-a-sp-monogram/` rather than deleted.

### Approved — "S + Spot / Access Point"

A geometric letter "S," built from two stacked semicircular arcs (not a typed font character — see §12 for the exact construction), whose top terminus resolves into a solid filled dot instead of a plain rounded end. The stroke is Oil Green; the dot is Amber. Reading the mark: the path *arrives* somewhere — the dot is the spot the S leads into or begins from — carrying the spot/access/arrival idea from the brief's naming logic (§3) without drawing a literal map pin, and without being a generic geometric swirl that could belong to any company starting with S.

**Hard constraint honored:** this is not a generic map-pin icon next to text, and it is not a typed-font monogram — it's custom geometric construction, verified at favicon scale before being treated as final (§14).

### Concept A — "The Access Point" (approved, then superseded — kept for record)

A rounded square — representing *the spot*, a space, a room, a place with edges — with a smaller circle nested and slightly overlapping at its lower-right corner — representing *arrival*, the moment of gaining access. Reviewed favorably in guideline form, but did not hold up once built into finished artwork; superseded by the mark above. Archived files: `docs/brand/assets/archive/concept-a-sp-monogram/`.

### Concept B — "The Open Corner" (not selected — kept for record)

A rounded-square outline left deliberately open at one corner — like a viewfinder or a bracket — with a small solid dot positioned just outside the opening, as if about to enter. More minimal and more "modern SaaS," at some cost to the warmth Concept A carried. Reviewed alongside Concept A and not chosen; retained here only so the alternative that was considered stays on record.

### Two other S-based directions considered (not selected)

- **Minimal Geometric S** — the same S curve alone, no dot, no container. Scored lowest of the three on favicon/app-icon fit and marketplace feel: without a second color or a fixed silhouette, it reads as a generic abstract initial rather than something specifically Spotva's.
- **S in a Contrasting Container** — the S curve inside a rounded-square tile, app-icon style. Scored highest of the three on pure icon-format metrics (it's already the shape every home-screen icon uses), and remains a strong fallback if a future context needs a stronger fixed-tile silhouette than the un-contained mark provides — but the brand owner chose the dot-terminus direction as the primary mark.

### Wordmark

"Spotva," set in Fraunces (§16), sentence case, unified — **never split, recolored, or re-weighted between "Spot" and "va"** anywhere public-facing, per the brief's explicit instruction (§3). The icon mark pairs with the wordmark as a **separate, combinable element** — placed to the left of the wordmark in the primary horizontal lockup (§11), scaled so its height matches the wordmark's cap-height (§12) — rather than being fused into a letterform. The icon's geometric construction and the wordmark's serif construction are deliberately different registers (this was already true of Concept A); this keeps the wordmark legible at small sizes and keeps the icon usable completely on its own (favicon, social avatar, app icon).

## 11. Logo Variations

All variations are the same mark, reproduced faithfully — never redrawn or reinterpreted per-context (§24 Do/Don't). Required set, all present in `docs/brand/assets/`:

1. **Primary horizontal lockup** — icon mark + "Spotva" wordmark, icon to the left, on light backgrounds (`logo-horizontal-light.svg`). Default logo for nearly everything.
2. **Icon-only mark** — the S+dot glyph alone (`icon-mark.svg`). Used wherever the wordmark won't stay legible: favicon, app icon, social avatar, small UI placements.
3. **Light-background version** — full color (Oil Green S + Amber dot + Oil Green wordmark) for use on white/cream/light-neutral surfaces (`icon-mark.svg`, `logo-horizontal-light.svg`).
4. **Dark-background version** — Cream S (an Oil Green S fails contrast against an Oil Green/near-black background — confirmed by render, not assumed) + Amber dot + Cream wordmark, for use on Oil Green or near-black surfaces (`icon-mark-on-dark.svg`, `logo-horizontal-dark.svg`).
5. **Monochrome version** — single-ink dark (`icon-mark-mono.svg`, `logo-horizontal-mono.svg`) and single-ink light (`icon-mark-mono-on-dark.svg`, `logo-horizontal-mono-on-dark.svg`) versions — both the S and the dot render in one color in these, losing the two-tone accent by design — for contexts where color reproduction isn't reliable (fax-era print vendors still exist; stamped/engraved goods; single-color merch).
6. **Favicon** — icon-only mark, favicon-specific cut with tighter padding than the general icon-only mark (`favicon.svg`), verified legible at true 16×16 and 32×32px before being finalized (§14).
7. **Social media avatar** — icon-only mark, centered with generous internal padding, on an Oil Green field (square and circular crops both need checking, since platforms crop avatars differently). **Not yet exported** — still to do.
8. **High-resolution transparent PNG** — every variant above, exported at a resolution suitable for large-format use. **Not yet exported** — still to do; the SVGs are the current source of truth.
9. **SVG master** — the source of truth for every other format; all raster exports are generated from it, not redrawn.

## 12. Logo Construction — ✅ verified by render

Construction logic for the S+dot mark, kept reproducible and consistent across every deliverable rather than redrawn by feel each time. Built with a script (not hand-drawn) directly from these rules, so every file in `docs/brand/assets/` shares identical geometry:

- **The S curve:** two semicircular bowls of radius **R**, sharing a vertical spine, stroked with round caps at stroke width **4/3 × R**. The top bowl bulges left (opens right, like a backwards "C" across the top); the bottom bowl bulges right (opens left, like a "C" across the bottom) — the standard two-arc construction for a geometric "S." In the shipped files, R = 15 units, stroke = 20 units, on a 62×92 (icon-only) viewBox.
- **The dot:** radius = **0.55 × stroke width**, centered on the S curve's top terminus, replacing its round cap. Sized deliberately smaller than the bowl radius — an earlier pass at ~0.78× stroke width swallowed the top curve and read as a lollipop rather than an S with an accent.
- **Icon-only files:** even padding on all sides (6 units at this scale) around the S+dot glyph.
- **Favicon-specific cut:** the same geometry with tighter padding (3 units) to maximize ink coverage at very small sizes — verified separately (§14), not assumed to scale down cleanly from the general icon-only file.
- **Horizontal lockup:** the icon is scaled uniformly so its height matches the wordmark's cap-height (96 units in the shipped files). Spacing between icon and wordmark is one icon-width, scaling proportionally with the mark rather than a fixed pixel value.

## 13. Clear Space

**Minimum protected space around the logo (icon+wordmark or icon alone):** no other element — text, image, edge of the page, another logo — inside a margin equal to the **cap-height of the wordmark's "S"** (or, for the icon-only mark, the height of the icon itself), measured on all four sides. This scales with the logo's size rather than being a fixed pixel value, so it holds true from a business card to a billboard.

## 14. Minimum Size — ✅ verified by render

- **Digital, horizontal lockup:** no smaller than 120px wide — verified by rendering `logo-horizontal-light.svg` at true 120px width; both the icon and "Spotva" in Fraunces stay legible at that size.
- **Digital, icon-only:** no smaller than 24×24px in general UI. Below that, use the favicon-specific cut (`favicon.svg`, 16×16/32×32) — verified by rendering at true pixel size (not scaled down from a larger render) and inspecting at 8× nearest-neighbor magnification; the S+dot relationship stayed legible at both sizes, including true 16×16.
- **Print, horizontal lockup:** no smaller than 25mm wide. *(Carried over from the previous proposal — not re-verified against print output, since nothing has gone to print yet.)*
- **Print, icon-only:** no smaller than 8mm. *(Same caveat as above.)*

## 16. Typography — ✅ APPROVED

**Confirmed, not replaced** — per the brief's instruction not to discard the existing direction without cause. Both faces are on Google Fonts and support the full AZ/EN/RU/TR/DE/ES character set (Golos Text in particular ships full Cyrillic coverage, which is why it's the UI face — see §25).

- **Display / brand headings:** **Fraunces** — a warm, characterful serif with old-style detail; carries the "premium but not corporate" personality (§8) in a way a geometric sans can't. Used for H1/H2, the wordmark, and hero moments. Not used for body text or UI chrome — its detail is a liability at small sizes and in dense interfaces.
- **UI / body / navigation:** **Golos Text** — a humanist grotesque built with Cyrillic as a first-class citizen, not a retrofit. Used for everything else: body copy, buttons, forms, navigation, tables, captions.

### 16.1 Type Scale — ✅ APPROVED (satisfies `08_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §8.1's "6–7 step scale" requirement)

| Role | Face | Size / Line-height (desktop) | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Fraunces | 40px / 48px | 600 (SemiBold) |
| H2 | Fraunces | 32px / 40px | 600 |
| H3 | Fraunces | 24px / 32px | 500 (Medium) |
| H4 | Golos Text | 20px / 28px | 600 |
| Body | Golos Text | 16px / 24px | 400 |
| Small | Golos Text | 14px / 20px | 400 |
| Labels / buttons | Golos Text | 14px / 20px | 600, slight uppercase tracking on labels only (not buttons) |
| Navigation | Golos Text | 15px / 24px | 500 |
| Captions | Golos Text | 12px / 16px | 400 |

### 16.2 Internationalization Check

Both faces need verification at implementation time (not assumed) for: Azerbaijani-specific characters (Ə/ə, Ğ/ğ, İ/ı, Ö/ö, Ü/ü), full Cyrillic (RU), Turkish-specific characters (İ/ı, Ğ/ğ, Ş/ş), and German (ß, umlauts). Golos Text's stated Cyrillic-first design makes RU the least risky; Fraunces is used sparingly enough (headings only) that any gap can be spot-checked rather than blocking the whole system. Per `08_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §8.4, components must also tolerate the 30–40% text-length expansion RU/DE routinely produce versus EN — this is a layout rule, not a typography one, and it already applies regardless of which face is chosen.

## 17. Iconography

A simple, geometric, consistent icon system that visually echoes the logo mark's own vocabulary (§10) — rounded squares and circles — rather than pulling from a generic mixed icon pack. Proposed spec: 24×24px grid, 2px stroke weight, rounded caps and joins, consistent corner radius matching the logo's own rounding ratio.

**Required category icons** (spans the current provider categories): meeting, classroom, training, office, coworking, event, workshop, podcast, creative space. Each icon should be legible as a solid glyph at 16px (nav/list contexts) and as a stroke icon at 24px+ (cards, empty states, category pages) — build both from the start rather than only discovering the small-size failure later.

---

## 18. Photography Direction

Spotva shows spaces **as places where things happen**, not as empty real-estate listings. Emphasize: real people, real spaces, activity, collaboration, learning, meetings, creativity, events, natural light, authentic environments. Avoid generic corporate stock photography wherever a real or realistic alternative exists.

Guiding line for anyone commissioning or selecting photography: **"This is where your next thing happens."** A photo passes that test if it shows a moment mid-use — a class in session, a meeting underway, a shoot being set up — not a staged, empty room shot from the doorway.

---

## 19. Voice & Tone

Concise, confident, welcoming, practical, human. Say the thing, not the hype around the thing.

**Avoid entirely:** "revolutionizing the future of work," "disrupting the industry," "next-generation ecosystem," and anything in that register.

**Prefer direct, verb-led language:** *Find a space. Book a room. Meet your team. Teach your class. Host your workshop. Find your spot.*

This tone rule applies everywhere copy appears — marketing pages, in-product microcopy, transactional emails, partner materials — and it's the same standard §7's brand promise implicitly sets: the brand talks the way a genuinely useful tool talks, not the way it markets itself.

## 21. Partner Branding

Partners (bloggers, influencers, marketing specialists, community owners, content creators, education/business communities) promote Spotva through referral links (`/r/{PARTNER_CODE}`, per `31_PARTNER_REFERRAL_ARCHITECTURE.md`) and earn commission on attributable bookings.

**Rule: partners use the Spotva master brand — no separate visual identity for the partner program.** Where partner-specific materials need their own label (a partner dashboard header, a partner-facing one-pager, an application page), use a secondary lockup: **"Spotva Partners"** or **"Spotva Partner Program"** — the Spotva wordmark/icon unchanged, with "Partners" set in Golos Text beneath or beside it, never redrawn into the mark itself. This keeps every partner-shared link and asset instantly recognizable as Spotva, which is also what makes it easy for a partner to promote credibly (§ brief point 18) — a partner-only sub-brand would do the opposite.

## 22. Social Media Usage

- **Avatar:** icon-only mark (§11.2/§11.7), centered on an Oil Green field, checked in both square and circular crop (platforms differ).
- **Cover/header images:** primary horizontal lockup, light-background version by default; dark-background version where the platform's own chrome is dark.
- **In-post graphics:** Fraunces for any headline text baked into an image, Golos Text for supporting text, drawn from the §15 palette — never an ad-hoc color introduced for one campaign.
- Photography follows §18 throughout — no generic stock in paid or organic social content where a real shot is available.

## 23. Advertising Usage

- CTA buttons/links in any ad creative use `amber-600`/`amber-700` exactly as specified in §15.2 — including the dark-text rule; white text on amber is a contrast failure regardless of format (display ad, social ad, print).
- Ad headlines in Fraunces, body/disclaimer text in Golos Text — consistent with §16, not a lighter "ad-safe" font substitution.
- No stretched, recolored, or cropped-into-an-unapproved-shape logo in any placement, including small display-ad formats — use the icon-only mark (§11.2) below the horizontal lockup's minimum size (§14) rather than shrinking the full lockup past legibility.

## 24. Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Use the SVG master (§11.9) and export from it | Redraw or trace the logo per use case |
| Keep "Spotva" as one visual unit | Split "Spot" and "va" by color, weight, or spacing anywhere public-facing (§3, §10) |
| Respect clear space (§13) at every size | Crowd the logo with text, other logos, or page edges |
| Scale the lockup proportionally | Stretch or squeeze the logo to fit a space |
| Use the provided rotation-free orientations only | Rotate or tilt the logo |
| Use §15's approved palette exactly | Recolor the logo arbitrarily, including "just this once" campaign colors |
| Keep the mark flat | Add drop shadows, bevels, or gradients without an explicit design-system update |
| Place the logo on backgrounds that clear contrast (§13–§15) | Place it on low-contrast or busy photographic backgrounds |
| Use standard tracking/kerning as delivered | Change letter-spacing on the wordmark |
| Keep icon + wordmark as a single combinable unit or fully separate | Break the lockup apart into new arrangements not covered by §11 |

## 25. Internationalization Considerations

- **Languages in scope:** Azerbaijani, English, Russian, Turkish, German, Spanish (per the brief and consistent with `20_I18N.md`).
- **Typography:** both Fraunces and Golos Text need character-set verification per §16.2 before final lock-in — this is a checklist item for implementation, not a decision blocked on this document.
- **Tagline:** §9 already flags Direction A's idiom risk in translation; resolve before using Direction A outside AZ/EN.
- **Layout tolerance:** `08_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §8.4's existing rule (RU/DE routinely run 30–40% longer than EN for equivalent strings) applies to every brand-driven UI surface — nav labels, button text, category names — not just body copy.
- **Color meaning:** the semantic palette (§15.6) uses conventional green/amber/red/blue associations, which hold reasonably well across all six target markets; no market-specific semantic recoloring is anticipated, but this is worth a light sanity check once the brand expands beyond AZ/EN/RU/TR.
- **Name/pronunciation:** "Spotva" was checked in the brief against the geographic-misreading risk (§3 — "VA ≠ Azerbaijan") and against not needing to be spelled out as an acronym publicly. No further linguistic clearance work is scoped in this document; if expansion into DE/ES markets is imminent, a basic negative-connotation check in those languages is a reasonable pre-launch step.
